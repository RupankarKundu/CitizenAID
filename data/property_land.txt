BUYING PROPERTY — LEGAL CHECKLIST
Before buying any property:
1. Check title deed — verify seller is actual owner (check for last 30 years)
2. Check encumbrance certificate from Sub-Registrar — shows all loans/mortgages on property
3. Check property tax receipts — ensure no dues pending
4. Check approved building plan (for constructed property)
5. Check Occupancy Certificate (OC) for apartments
6. Check for litigation — search court records
7. Verify land use: residential/commercial/agricultural — check with local planning authority
8. For agricultural land: check if conversion to non-agricultural use is done

PROPERTY REGISTRATION — MANDATORY
Any property transaction above Rs. 100 must be registered. Unregistered sale deed is not valid for transferring title. Registration must happen within 4 months of execution of deed. Stamp duty and registration charges vary by state (typically 5-7% of property value).

HOW TO REGISTER PROPERTY
Step 1: Execute sale deed on stamp paper of required value
Step 2: Both buyer and seller visit Sub-Registrar's office with original documents
Step 3: Pay stamp duty and registration fee
Step 4: Documents verified and registered
Step 5: Collect registered document — this is your title deed

PROPERTY MUTATION (DAKHIL KHARIJ)
After buying property, get mutation done in revenue records:
- Apply at local Municipal Corporation or Revenue/Tehsil office
- Submit: registered sale deed, property tax receipts, identity proof
- Mutation in revenue records establishes your name as owner for tax purposes
- Without mutation, property tax still comes in previous owner's name
- File RTI if mutation is delayed without reason

LAND RECORDS — HOW TO CHECK
- Khatauni (Record of Rights): shows who owns the land and what they own
- Khasra/Plot map: shows boundaries of the land
- Check online: most states have digitized land records
  - West Bengal: banglarbhumi.gov.in
  - Maharashtra: mahabhulekh.maharashtra.gov.in
  - Delhi: dlrc.delhigovt.nic.in
  - UP: upbhulekh.gov.in
  - Karnataka: landrecords.karnataka.gov.in

ENCROACHMENT ON YOUR LAND
If someone has illegally occupied your land:
Step 1: File FIR at local police station under Section 441 IPC (criminal trespass)
Step 2: File civil suit for recovery of possession in civil court
Step 3: Apply for injunction to prevent further construction
Step 4: File complaint with Revenue/Tehsil officer for action on encroachment
Step 5: File RTI to get land records and mutation details

PROPERTY DISPUTES WITH FAMILY
- If ancestral property: all legal heirs have equal rights
- Partition suit: any co-owner can file suit for partition in civil court
- Father cannot disinherit children from ancestral property
- Self-acquired property: owner can give to anyone through will

INHERITANCE AND SUCCESSION
Hindu Succession Act 1956 (amended 2005):
- Daughter has equal right as son in ancestral property
- Class I heirs: son, daughter, widow, mother get equal share
- If no will: property divided equally among legal heirs
- Muslim inheritance: governed by personal law — consult a lawyer
- Christian inheritance: Indian Succession Act 1925

WILL — HOW TO MAKE A VALID WILL
- Must be in writing
- Must be signed by testator (person making will)
- Must be signed by 2 witnesses (who are not beneficiaries)
- Registration not mandatory but recommended
- Can be changed anytime during lifetime
- Registered at Sub-Registrar's office

BENAMI PROPERTY
Benami Transactions (Prohibition) Amendment Act 2016:
- Property held in another person's name is benami
- Benami property can be confiscated by government
- Punishment: 1-7 years imprisonment + fine up to 25% of property value
- Informers can report at income tax department

GOVERNMENT LAND ACQUISITION
Right to Fair Compensation Act 2013:
- Government must conduct Social Impact Assessment before acquisition
- Compensation: 2x market value in urban, 3x-4x in rural areas
- Consent required: 80% for private companies, 70% for PPP projects
- If not used within 5 years, land must be returned
- Challenge acquisition in High Court if procedure not followed

PROPERTY TAX
- Property tax is levied by local body (municipal corporation, panchayat)
- Pay annually or as demanded — check with your local municipal body
- Property tax receipt is important proof of possession
- Challenge wrong assessment before Assessment Officer of municipal body
- Many municipalities allow online payment and self-assessment

HOMEBUYER RIGHTS AGAINST BUILDERS (RERA)
Real Estate Regulation Act 2016:
- Builder must register project with State RERA before selling
- Buyer has right to full project details: plans, approvals, completion date
- Builder must deposit 70% of collected amount in separate escrow account
- Delay in possession: builder must pay interest on deposited amount
- Structural defects: builder liable for 5 years after possession
- File complaint at State RERA portal — no lawyer needed for basic complaints
- RERA Portals: maharera.mahaonline.gov.in (Maharashtra), haryanarera.gov.in (Haryana), etc.
