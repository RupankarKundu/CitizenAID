RIGHT TO EDUCATION (RTE ACT 2009)

FREE AND COMPULSORY EDUCATION
Every child aged 6-14 years has the fundamental right to free and compulsory education (Article 21A, Constitution). No child can be denied admission or charged fees in government schools. Private unaided schools must reserve 25% seats for children from economically weaker sections (EWS) and disadvantaged groups — admission is free.

ADMISSION RIGHTS
- No child can be refused admission to a neighbourhood school
- No admission test or interview allowed for admission to Class 1
- Age proof: birth certificate OR declaration from parent if no birth certificate
- No child can be held back or expelled till completion of Class 8
- No corporal punishment allowed — teachers can be punished for hitting students

25% EWS RESERVATION IN PRIVATE SCHOOLS
Who qualifies: annual family income below Rs. 1 lakh (varies by state) or belonging to SC/ST/OBC or disabled children.
How to apply:
Step 1: Apply online at state education department RTE portal during admission season (usually Feb-March)
Step 2: Upload income certificate, caste certificate, residence proof, child's birth certificate
Step 3: Lottery system allocates seats
Step 4: School cannot charge any fee from EWS students — not even for books, uniform, bus
If school denies admission or charges fee: complaint to District Education Officer or State RTE helpline

MID-DAY MEAL (PM POSHAN)
Every government school student from Class 1-8 entitled to free cooked meal. Meal must meet nutritional standards. If mid-day meal not provided: complaint to District Education Officer. If food quality poor: complaint to Block Education Officer.

SCHOLARSHIPS AND GOVERNMENT SCHEMES FOR EDUCATION
- Pre-Matric Scholarship: for SC/ST/OBC/Minority students — apply at scholarships.gov.in
- Post-Matric Scholarship: for Class 11 onwards SC/ST/OBC/Minority students
- National Means-cum-Merit Scholarship (NMMS): for Class 8 students from low-income families
- Kasturba Gandhi Balika Vidyalaya: free residential schools for girls from weaker sections
- Beti Bachao Beti Padhao: various schemes for girl child education
Apply: scholarships.gov.in or National Scholarship Portal

POCSO ACT 2012 — PROTECTION OF CHILDREN FROM SEXUAL OFFENCES

WHO IS A CHILD
Any person below 18 years of age.

WHAT IS COVERED
- Sexual assault: penetration without consent — minimum 7 years to life imprisonment
- Sexual harassment: unwelcome physical contact, demand for sexual favour, showing pornography — 3 years imprisonment
- Stalking of child: 3 years imprisonment
- Using child for pornography: 5 years minimum imprisonment

MANDATORY REPORTING
Every person who has knowledge of a POCSO offence MUST report it to local police or Special Juvenile Police Unit (SJPU). Failure to report is a punishable offence. Teachers, doctors, and all professionals are specifically bound to report.

HOW TO REPORT POCSO OFFENCE
Step 1: Call 1098 (CHILDLINE) — 24-hour helpline, free
Step 2: Call 100 (Police) or 112 (Emergency)
Step 3: Visit nearest police station — they must record complaint without delay
Step 4: FIR must be registered — police cannot refuse
Step 5: Child's statement recorded by lady police officer
Step 6: Medical examination done with child's/parent's consent
Step 7: Case tried in Special Court — child's identity protected

CHILD'S RIGHTS DURING INVESTIGATION
- Child does not need to go to police station for statement if traumatised
- Statement recorded at home or comfortable place
- Identity of child cannot be disclosed publicly
- Child cannot be detained in police station
- Free legal aid provided to child and family

CHILD LABOUR PROHIBITION
Child Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) Amendment Act 2016:
- Complete ban on employment of children below 14 years in any occupation
- Ban on employment of children aged 14-18 in hazardous occupations (mines, explosive factories, etc.)
- Punishment: 6 months to 2 years imprisonment for employers
- If you see a child working: call 1098 (CHILDLINE) or file complaint with Labour Department

JUVENILE JUSTICE (CARE AND PROTECTION OF CHILDREN) ACT 2015
- Children in conflict with law (those who commit crimes) treated differently from adults
- Children below 16 tried as juvenile even for serious crimes (exceptions for 16-18 in heinous crimes)
- Focus on rehabilitation not punishment
- Child Welfare Committees (CWC) in each district for children in need of care
- Shelter Homes for destitute/abandoned children

ADOPTION
- Hindu Adoption under Hindu Adoption and Maintenance Act
- Non-Hindu adoption under Juvenile Justice Act through CARA (Central Adoption Resource Authority)
- Register on CARA portal: cara.nic.in
- Process takes 1-2 years
- Single parents can also adopt

EDUCATION HELPLINES AND PORTALS
- CHILDLINE: 1098 (24 hours, free)
- RTE complaints: State Education Department
- Scholarship portal: scholarships.gov.in
- POCSO e-box (online reporting): posco.ncpcr.gov.in
- National Commission for Protection of Child Rights: ncpcr.gov.in
- Cybercrime against children: cybercrime.gov.in (specific section)
