STARTING A BUSINESS — WHICH STRUCTURE TO CHOOSE

SOLE PROPRIETORSHIP
Simplest form. You and the business are the same legal entity.
- No registration mandatory (but recommended — shop license, GST, etc.)
- Unlimited personal liability — your personal assets at risk if business fails
- No separate tax — income taxed as personal income
- Best for: very small businesses, freelancers, local shops
- Register: Get Udyam Registration (MSME), Shop & Establishment license from local municipality, GST if turnover above Rs. 20 lakhs

PARTNERSHIP FIRM
2 or more persons running business together.
- Register under Indian Partnership Act 1932 at Registrar of Firms
- Partnership deed must specify: profit sharing ratio, roles, dispute resolution
- Unlimited liability for all partners
- If partner cheats: file civil suit for breach of partnership deed + criminal complaint under Section 405 IPC (criminal breach of trust)
- Dissolve at any time by mutual consent or court order

LIMITED LIABILITY PARTNERSHIP (LLP)
Best for small professional firms — lawyers, CAs, consultants.
- Registered at MCA portal: mca.gov.in
- Limited liability — personal assets protected
- Minimum 2 partners, no maximum
- Annual filing required with MCA
- LLP Agreement governs the business
- Cost to register: Rs. 500-5,000 depending on capital

PRIVATE LIMITED COMPANY (PVT LTD)
Most popular for startups and growing businesses.
- Registered under Companies Act 2013 at MCA: mca.gov.in
- Minimum 2 directors, minimum 2 shareholders
- Limited liability — shareholders only lose their investment
- Can raise investment from investors
- Must file annual returns, financial statements with MCA
- Audit mandatory if turnover exceeds threshold
- Cost to register: Rs. 6,000-15,000 (government fees)

ONE PERSON COMPANY (OPC)
For solo entrepreneurs who want limited liability.
- Single person can form a company
- Must nominate one person as nominee director
- Limited liability protection
- Annual compliance required

PUBLIC LIMITED COMPANY
For larger businesses wanting to raise public money.
- Minimum 7 shareholders, 3 directors
- Can list on stock exchange (BSE/NSE) after IPO
- Heavy compliance requirements under SEBI and Companies Act
- Regulated by SEBI if listed

HOW TO REGISTER A COMPANY — STEP BY STEP
Step 1: Get Digital Signature Certificate (DSC) for directors — from authorized agencies, costs Rs. 1,000-2,000
Step 2: Apply for Director Identification Number (DIN) — free on MCA portal
Step 3: Reserve company name — apply on MCA portal (RUN form), costs Rs. 1,000
Step 4: Draft Memorandum of Association (MOA) and Articles of Association (AOA)
Step 5: File SPICe+ form on MCA portal with all documents
Step 6: Pay stamp duty and registration fee
Step 7: Certificate of Incorporation issued — your company is born
Step 8: Apply for PAN, TAN, GST registration, bank account

All done at: mca.gov.in
Helpline: 1800-200-5522 (MCA)

MSME REGISTRATION (UDYAM REGISTRATION)

WHO IS AN MSME
- Micro: Investment up to Rs. 1 crore AND turnover up to Rs. 5 crore
- Small: Investment up to Rs. 10 crore AND turnover up to Rs. 50 crore
- Medium: Investment up to Rs. 50 crore AND turnover up to Rs. 250 crore

BENEFITS OF UDYAM REGISTRATION (FREE)
- Priority bank loans at lower interest rates
- Government tenders reserved for MSMEs
- Protection against delayed payments (MSME Samadhaan)
- Subsidies and schemes from central/state government
- 1% interest subvention on loans up to Rs. 1 crore
- Free ISO certification assistance
Register at: udyamregistration.gov.in — just need Aadhaar and PAN

MSME PAYMENT PROTECTION — VERY IMPORTANT
If a large company owes you money and is delaying payment:
- Under MSMED Act 2006: payment must be made within 45 days if agreed, or 15 days if no agreement
- If not paid: buyer must pay compound interest at 3x bank rate
- File complaint at MSME Samadhaan portal: samadhaan.dcmsme.gov.in
- Facilitation Council will mediate and can award payment with interest
- This is one of the strongest protections for small businesses

COMPANY LAW — RIGHTS OF SHAREHOLDERS

MINORITY SHAREHOLDER RIGHTS
Even if you hold small percentage of shares you have rights:
- Right to attend and vote at general meetings
- Right to inspect company books and registers
- Right to receive dividends if declared
- Right to receive annual report
- Protection against oppression and mismanagement — Section 241-244 Companies Act

OPPRESSION AND MISMANAGEMENT
If majority shareholders are:
- Running company for their own benefit at expense of minority
- Committing fraud or misappropriating company funds
- Acting in manner prejudicial to public interest
File petition before National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) under Section 241 Companies Act. Minority shareholders holding 10%+ shares can file directly.

NATIONAL COMPANY LAW TRIBUNAL (NCLT)
NCLT handles:
- Oppression and mismanagement cases
- Insolvency and bankruptcy cases
- Company mergers and demergers
- Winding up of companies
- Appeals against company law violations
File at NCLT bench for your region. No lawyer mandatory but recommended.

INSOLVENCY AND BANKRUPTCY CODE (IBC) 2016

IF A COMPANY OWES YOU MONEY (OPERATIONAL CREDITOR)
If a company has not paid you for goods supplied or services rendered:
Step 1: Send demand notice under Section 8 IBC — company has 10 days to pay or dispute
Step 2: If no payment: file application before NCLT under Section 9
Step 3: NCLT admits application if debt above Rs. 1 crore (earlier Rs. 1 lakh)
Step 4: Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP) begins
Step 5: Resolution Professional takes over management of company
Step 6: Company either restructures and pays creditors, or goes into liquidation
Minimum claim: Rs. 1 crore for companies

IF YOU OWE MONEY AND CANNOT PAY (PERSONAL INSOLVENCY)
Individuals and personal guarantors: file under Part III IBC before Debt Recovery Tribunal (DRT). Fresh Start Process for individuals with income below Rs. 60,000 and assets below Rs. 20,000. Repayment Plan process for others.

DIRECTOR'S LIABILITIES AND DISQUALIFICATION

WHEN DIRECTORS ARE PERSONALLY LIABLE
Directors can be held personally liable for:
- Fraudulent trading — continuing business knowing company cannot pay debts
- Wrongful trading — taking on new debts when insolvent
- Non-filing of returns for 3 consecutive years — automatic disqualification
- Not maintaining proper books of accounts
- Environmental violations by the company

DIRECTOR DISQUALIFICATION
A disqualified director cannot be director of any company for 5 years:
- Convicted of offence related to promotion, formation, management of company
- Found guilty of fraud or breach of duty
- Company does not file financial statements or annual returns for 3 consecutive years
Check director disqualification: mca.gov.in

ANNUAL COMPLIANCE FOR COMPANIES

MANDATORY FILINGS (PRIVATE LIMITED COMPANY)
Every year without fail:
- Form AOC-4: Annual financial statements — within 30 days of AGM
- Form MGT-7: Annual return — within 60 days of AGM
- ADT-1: Appointment of auditor (once every 5 years)
- DIR-3 KYC: Director KYC — every year by September 30
- Income Tax Return: by October 31 for companies

PENALTY FOR NON-COMPLIANCE
- Late filing: Rs. 100 per day per form (no maximum cap — can become lakhs)
- Strike off: if company does not file for 2+ years, MCA can strike off the company
- Director disqualification: if company does not comply for 3 years

LABOUR LAWS FOR BUSINESSES

SMALL BUSINESSES — KEY COMPLIANCE
Shops and Establishments Act (state-specific):
- Register your shop/office within 30 days of starting
- Register at local municipality or labor department
- Get Shops and Establishments Certificate
- Governs: working hours, holidays, leave, overtime

If you employ workers, mandatory compliance:
- PF registration if 20+ employees: epfindia.gov.in
- ESI registration if 10+ employees: esic.gov.in
- Professional Tax registration (state-specific)
- Payment of Gratuity Act if 10+ employees
- Minimum wages as notified by state

CONTRACT LAW — BASICS (INDIAN CONTRACT ACT 1872)

VALID CONTRACT REQUIREMENTS
For a contract to be legally enforceable:
1. Offer and acceptance
2. Consideration (something of value exchanged)
3. Capacity of parties (both must be adults, sound mind)
4. Free consent (no coercion, fraud, or misrepresentation)
5. Lawful object
6. Not expressly declared void

IF SOMEONE BREACHES CONTRACT
- Send legal notice demanding performance or compensation
- Mediation/arbitration if contract has such clause
- File civil suit for specific performance or damages in civil court
- For smaller amounts: Consumer Forum if service-related, or Small Causes Court

ARBITRATION CLAUSE IN CONTRACTS
Many business contracts have arbitration clause — disputes must go to arbitrator, not court. Arbitration is faster and cheaper than court. Governed by Arbitration and Conciliation Act 1996. If you lose: appeal in High Court only on limited grounds.

GST FOR BUSINESSES

GST REGISTRATION — WHEN MANDATORY
- Turnover above Rs. 20 lakhs (Rs. 10 lakhs for special category states)
- Interstate supply regardless of turnover
- E-commerce sellers regardless of turnover
- Certain other specified categories
Register at: gst.gov.in (free)

GST COMPLIANCE
- File GSTR-1 (sales): monthly or quarterly
- File GSTR-3B (summary): monthly
- Annual return: GSTR-9 by December 31
Penalty for non-filing: Rs. 50/day (Rs. 20/day for nil returns), up to Rs. 5,000

GST FRAUD AND ITC MISUSE
Input Tax Credit (ITC) fraud is a serious offence:
- Claiming ITC on fake invoices: penalty equal to tax amount + imprisonment up to 5 years
- Report GST fraud: gst.gov.in or call 1800-103-4786

INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY FOR BUSINESSES

TRADEMARK REGISTRATION
Protect your brand name, logo:
- Register at Intellectual Property India: ipindia.gov.in
- Cost: Rs. 4,500 per class (small enterprise), Rs. 9,000 (others)
- Protection for 10 years, renewable
- Unregistered trademark still has common law protection
- If someone copies your trademark: send legal notice + file complaint at IP India + civil suit

COPYRIGHT
Automatic protection from creation — no registration needed. Registration recommended as evidence. Register at: copyright.gov.in. Infringement: civil damages + criminal punishment up to 3 years.

PATENT
For new inventions:
- File at IP India: ipindia.gov.in
- Protection for 20 years
- Must be novel, inventive, industrially applicable
- Cost: Rs. 1,600 (natural person/startup), Rs. 4,000 (small entity)

STARTUP INDIA BENEFITS
Register as startup at startupindia.gov.in:
- Tax holiday: 3 years income tax exemption in first 10 years
- Fast-track patent examination
- Self-certification for 9 labour laws for 5 years
- Rs. 10,000 crore fund of funds for investment
- Easy winding up within 90 days under IBC
Eligibility: company up to 10 years old, annual turnover below Rs. 100 crore, working on innovation
