PART III - FUNDAMENTAL RIGHTS (Articles 12-35)

ARTICLE 14 - RIGHT TO EQUALITY
The State shall not deny to any person equality before the law or the equal protection of the laws within the territory of India. This means every citizen can demand equal treatment from government authorities. If a government official is selectively applying rules or discriminating without valid reason, you can challenge this under Article 14.

ARTICLE 19 - FREEDOM OF SPEECH AND EXPRESSION
All citizens shall have the right to freedom of speech and expression; to assemble peaceably and without arms; to form associations or unions or co-operative societies; to move freely throughout the territory of India; to reside and settle in any part of India; to practise any profession, or to carry on any occupation, trade or business. These rights are subject to reasonable restrictions.

ARTICLE 21 - RIGHT TO LIFE AND PERSONAL LIBERTY
No person shall be deprived of his life or personal liberty except according to procedure established by law. The Supreme Court has expanded Article 21 to include: right to livelihood, right to health, right to education, right to clean environment, right to privacy (Justice Puttaswamy case 2017), right to dignity, right against torture in custody, right to speedy trial.

ARTICLE 21A - RIGHT TO EDUCATION
The State shall provide free and compulsory education to all children of the age of six to fourteen years in such manner as the State may determine (added by 86th Amendment). RTE Act 2009 implements this — private schools must reserve 25% seats for economically weaker sections.

ARTICLE 22 - PROTECTION AGAINST ARBITRARY ARREST
No person who is arrested shall be detained in custody without being informed, as soon as may be, of the grounds for such arrest. Every person who is arrested must be produced before the nearest magistrate within 24 hours. No person shall be denied the right to consult a legal practitioner of his choice. This article protects against illegal detention.

ARTICLE 23 - PROHIBITION OF TRAFFIC IN HUMAN BEINGS
Traffic in human beings, beggar and other similar forms of forced labour are prohibited and any contravention of this provision shall be an offence punishable in accordance with law.

ARTICLE 24 - PROHIBITION OF CHILD LABOUR
No child below the age of fourteen years shall be employed in any factory or mine or engaged in any other hazardous employment.

ARTICLE 25-28 - FREEDOM OF RELIGION
Every person has freedom of conscience and the right to freely profess, practise and propagate religion. No person shall be compelled to pay any taxes for promotion or maintenance of any particular religion.

ARTICLE 29-30 - CULTURAL AND EDUCATIONAL RIGHTS
Any section of the citizens residing in the territory of India having a distinct language, script or culture shall have the right to conserve the same. Minorities have right to establish and administer educational institutions.

ARTICLE 32 - RIGHT TO CONSTITUTIONAL REMEDIES (Dr. Ambedkar called this the "Heart and Soul" of the Constitution)
Every citizen has the right to move the Supreme Court for enforcement of fundamental rights. The Supreme Court shall have power to issue writs (Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Quo Warranto, Certiorari) for enforcement of fundamental rights. This is itself a fundamental right.

WRITS EXPLAINED:
- HABEAS CORPUS: "Produce the body" — used when a person is illegally detained. Courts can order release.
- MANDAMUS: "We command" — order issued to a public authority to perform its public duty.
- PROHIBITION: Issued to lower courts to prevent exceeding jurisdiction.
- CERTIORARI: Issued to quash orders passed by inferior courts/tribunals acting without jurisdiction.
- QUO WARRANTO: Challenges unlawful assumption of public office.

ARTICLE 39A - FREE LEGAL AID
The State shall ensure equal justice and free legal aid. NALSA (National Legal Services Authority) provides free legal aid to: women, children, SC/ST persons, persons with disability, victims of trafficking, industrial workmen, persons in custody, persons with annual income below threshold. Call 15100 for free legal aid.

ARTICLE 226 - HIGH COURT WRIT JURISDICTION
High Courts can issue writs not just for fundamental rights but for "any other purpose" — much wider than Supreme Court. Anyone can approach their state High Court to enforce legal rights against government authorities.

DIRECTIVE PRINCIPLES (Part IV):
Though not enforceable in court, these guide government policy:
- Equal pay for equal work (Article 39(d))
- Right to adequate means of livelihood
- Maternity relief
- Free and compulsory education
- Living wage for workers
- Participation of workers in management

IMPORTANT LEGAL REMEDIES FOR CITIZENS:

PUBLIC INTEREST LITIGATION (PIL):
Any citizen can file a PIL in High Court or Supreme Court on behalf of the public when a constitutional right is violated. No locus standi requirement — even a letter to the court can be treated as PIL.

WRIT PETITION:
File in High Court (Article 226) or Supreme Court (Article 32) when fundamental rights are violated by government. No court fees for fundamental rights petitions.

GRIEVANCE PORTALS:
- CPGRAMS (Central Public Grievance Redress System): pgportal.gov.in
- PM's Office grievances: pmoini.gov.in
- State-specific CM portals

ANTI-CORRUPTION:
- Central Vigilance Commission: cvc.gov.in
- Lokpal: lokpal.gov.in
- State Lokayuktas for state government corruption
- CBI for corruption cases involving Central Government employees
- Anti-Corruption Bureau for state-level corruption

POLICE COMPLAINTS:
- If police refuses to register FIR, you can: file a written complaint to the Superintendent of Police; approach the Executive Magistrate; file a private complaint before the Judicial Magistrate under Section 156(3) CrPC; approach the State Human Rights Commission; file complaint with Police Complaints Authority.

LABOUR RIGHTS:
- Minimum wages must be paid as per state government notification
- Provident Fund (PF): employees earning up to Rs. 15,000 must get PF. File complaint at EPFO office or epfigms.gov.in
- ESI (Employee State Insurance): entitled to medical benefits if contributing
- Gratuity: entitled after 5 years of continuous service
- Labour Commissioner office handles disputes
