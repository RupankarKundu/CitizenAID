DOMESTIC VIOLENCE ACT 2005 — PROTECTION FOR WOMEN

WHO IS PROTECTED
Any woman in a domestic relationship including: wife, live-in partner, daughter, mother, sister, widow. The law covers relationships where parties live or have lived together in a shared household.

WHAT COUNTS AS DOMESTIC VIOLENCE
1. Physical abuse: hitting, slapping, kicking, pushing, burning, any physical hurt
2. Sexual abuse: forced sexual intercourse, sexual humiliation
3. Emotional/verbal abuse: insults, humiliation, threats, name-calling, taunting about dowry
4. Economic abuse: not providing money for basic needs, preventing from working, controlling finances, not giving maintenance
5. Dowry-related harassment: demand for dowry or any property by husband or in-laws

HOW TO GET PROTECTION — STEP BY STEP
Step 1: Contact a Protection Officer (PO) — available in every district. Find at District Collector's office.
Step 2: File Domestic Incident Report (DIR) with PO or directly approach Magistrate
Step 3: Can also file complaint directly with police under Section 498A IPC (cruelty by husband/in-laws)
Step 4: Court can immediately issue:
   - Protection Order: restraining abuser from contacting or coming near you
   - Residence Order: allowing you to stay in shared home, restraining abuser from evicting you
   - Monetary Relief: maintenance, medical expenses, loss of earnings
   - Custody Order: temporary custody of children
Step 5: Emergency orders can be passed same day

WHERE TO FILE COMPLAINT
- Protection Officer at District Collector's office
- Local police station (Women's cell preferred)
- Magistrate's court directly
- Registered NGOs authorised as Service Providers
Helpline: 181 (Women in Distress) — 24 hours, free

RIGHT TO STAY IN MATRIMONIAL HOME
Even if the home is not in the woman's name, she has the right to stay in the shared household. Husband or in-laws cannot evict her without court order.

SECTION 498A IPC — CRUELTY BY HUSBAND/IN-LAWS
Husband or relatives of husband subjecting a woman to cruelty — imprisonment up to 3 years + fine. Cruelty includes: harassment for dowry, mental cruelty, wilful conduct causing grave injury. This is a cognizable and non-bailable offence.

DOWRY PROHIBITION ACT 1961
Giving or taking dowry is illegal. Punishment: minimum 5 years imprisonment + minimum Rs. 15,000 fine. Demand for dowry: same punishment. File complaint with police or Dowry Prohibition Officer (appointed in each district).

DIVORCE — HINDU MARRIAGE ACT 1955

GROUNDS FOR DIVORCE (EITHER PARTY)
Cruelty, adultery, desertion (2 years), conversion to another religion, mental disorder, leprosy, venereal disease, renunciation of world, not heard of for 7 years.

MUTUAL CONSENT DIVORCE
- Both parties agree to divorce
- File joint petition in Family Court
- First motion: court passes order
- Wait 6 months (cooling off period) — can be waived by court
- Second motion: final divorce decree
- Fastest and least expensive route — usually 6-18 months

CONTESTED DIVORCE
- One party files divorce petition in Family Court
- Other party can contest
- Can take 2-7 years in court
- Grounds must be proven

MAINTENANCE (ALIMONY)
Section 125 CrPC: Husband must pay maintenance to wife if she cannot maintain herself. Wife can file application in Magistrate's court. Interim maintenance can be ordered within 60 days. Amount depends on husband's income and wife's needs. Maintenance continues until wife remarries or wife's income is sufficient.

CHILDREN — CUSTODY AND MAINTENANCE
- Child below 5 years: usually with mother
- Best interest of child is paramount consideration
- Father must pay maintenance for children regardless of custody
- Mother can also be ordered to pay if she has income
- Visitation rights given to non-custodial parent

DOMESTIC VIOLENCE AGAINST MEN
While DV Act specifically protects women, men can seek:
- Protection under general IPC sections if physically attacked
- Divorce on grounds of cruelty
- Custody of children
- Men's helpline: iCall 9152987821

WHAT TO DO IN DOMESTIC VIOLENCE EMERGENCY
1. Call 100 (police) or 112 (emergency) immediately
2. Leave with children if possible
3. Go to nearest police station — they must help you
4. Call 181 (Women Helpline)
5. Go to nearest government hospital for medical examination and evidence documentation
6. Contact Protection Officer next working day

MARRIAGE REGISTRATION
- All marriages should be registered
- Hindu Marriage: register at Sub-Registrar's office
- Court Marriage: under Special Marriage Act at Marriage Officer's office
- Unregistered marriage is still valid but registration provides legal proof
- Required documents: age proof, address proof, photos, witness

LIVE-IN RELATIONSHIPS
Supreme Court has ruled that long-term live-in relationships are equivalent to marriage. Partners have rights similar to spouses. Children born are legitimate. Women in live-in relationships are protected under Domestic Violence Act.
